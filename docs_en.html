CCPL Script Specification & Authoring Guide

Chemiation Chemical Principle Language · A declarative DSL for chemical mechanism deduction

1. Design Principles & Overview

CCPL (Chemiation Chemical Principle Language) is a declarative domain-specific language designed specifically for step-by-step chemical reaction mechanism deduction. Core design principles include:

  • Declarative & Readable: Natural, clean syntax without verbose JSON structures or boilerplate code.
  • Automatic Spatial Layout: You do not need to calculate 3D coordinates manually. Defining atoms and bond connectivity is sufficient; the layout algorithm calculates 3D molecular conformations.
  • Strict Mass Conservation: Fully tracks all atoms across elementary steps, eliminating disconnected or vanishing atoms.
  • Mechanism-Focused: Centers on bond cleavage, bond formation, and electron-pair migrations.

2. Global Reaction Metadata

The top of a CCPL script declares the reaction's overall metadata:

# Reaction title (required)
reaction "Fischer Esterification of Acetic Acid and Ethanol"

# Overall equation (required; supports standard keyboard inputs like -> and <=>)
equation "CH_3COOH + CH_3CH_2OH <=> CH_3COOCH_2CH_3 + H_2O"

# Mechanism summary and background (optional)
summary "Nucleophilic acyl substitution: reactant approach, tetrahedral intermediate formation, followed by elimination to form ethyl acetate and water."
💡 Subscript & LaTeX Notation: When writing equations, summaries, or step notes, there is no need to manually insert Unicode subscript characters:
  • Automatic Chemical Subscripts: Digits following element symbols or closing brackets (such as CH3COOH, H2O, CO2, C6H12O6) are automatically parsed into subscripts (CH₃COOH, H₂O, CO₂, C₆H₁₂O₆).
  • Explicit Subscript Syntax: Supports _2, _{10}, and _n (e.g. H_2O → H₂O, [C6H10O5]_n → [C₆H₁₀O₅]ₙ).
  • Explicit Superscript Syntax: Supports ^2, ^{2+}, ^+, and ^- for ionic charges (e.g. Fe^{3+} → Fe³⁺).
  • Arrow Shorthands: -> or --> converts to →, <=> or == converts to reversible equilibrium arrow ⇌, and <-> converts to resonance arrow ↔.

3. Elementary Reaction Steps (Step Block)

Each elementary step is enclosed within a step "Step Name" { ... } block. Manual step numbers (such as 1. or 2.) are not required; the engine numbers steps in their written order:

step "Reactant Collision and Approach" {
  # Mechanism explanation for this step
  note "Acetic acid and ethanol molecules approach under thermal motion; hydroxyl oxygen prepares to attack the carbonyl carbon."

  # Atom declarations
  atom C1 C -2.0 0.0 0.0
  atom O1 O -1.5 1.2 0.0
  
  # Bond declarations
  bond C1 O1 2
}
Tip: You do not need to write numbers in step titles; steps are numbered sequentially.

4. Atom and Bond Topology Definitions

4.1 Atom Definition (atom)

Two syntax forms are supported:

Syntax Format Description Use Case
atom <ID> <Element> <X> <Y> <Z> Explicit relative 3D coordinates Specific spatial orientations or high-precision symmetries
atom <ID> <Element> Omit coordinates; computed automatically Rapid drafting and automatic molecular layout (see Diels-Alder preset)

4.2 Bond Definition (bond)

Syntax: bond <AtomID1> <AtomID2> [Order]

  • bond C1 C2 1: Single bond between C1 and C2 (default).
  • bond C1 C2 1.5: Aromatic / resonance bond (order 1.5, rendered with parallel solid and dashed lines).
  • bond C1 O1 2: Double bond between C1 and O1 (e.g. carbonyl C=O).
  • bond N1 N2 3: Triple bond between N1 and N2 (e.g. dinitrogen N≡N).
💡 Note: Bonds are rendered with a smooth depth gradient reflecting both connected atoms and spatial depth.

4.3 Radicals (radical) and Formal Charges (charge)

Accurate notation of radical centers and ionic intermediates is essential in reaction mechanism deduction:

  • Radical Centers (radical): Denotes an unpaired electron (e.g. chlorine radical Cl·, methyl radical ·CH3). Rendered as a distinct dot near the atom symbol.
    Inline syntax: atom C1 C -1.8 0 0 radical
    Separate statement: radical C1 Cl2
  • Formal Charges (charge): Denotes ionic charges (e.g. oxonium O+, alkoxide O−, carbocation C+). Rendered as badge indicators (⊕ or ⊖).
    Inline syntax: atom O1 O -1.4 1.1 0 charge=-1 or atom O2 O -1.0 -1.3 0 charge=+1
    Separate statement: charge O1 -1, charge O2 +1
  • Charge Suppression in Polymers: When an atom is included within a polymer bracket, reactive charge badges are automatically suppressed to reflect unit closure.

4.4 Aromatic Hydrocarbons & Delocalized Π Systems (aromatic, Optional)

In aromatic rings or conjugated cyclic systems, delocalized Π bond visualization is completely optional. By specifying an aromatic (or short pi) statement inside a step block, the pseudo-3D renderer displays the classic Robinson inscribed circle and upper/lower delocalized π electron cloud lobes in 3D perspective:

# Syntax: aromatic "<ring atom IDs...>" [tag "<label>"]
aromatic "C1 C2 C3 C4 C5 C6" tag "Π_6^6"
  • Optional Design: Steps without an aromatic declaration render standard bonds normally (such as alternating double/single bonds or 1.5 order); the delocalized Π system is only projected when declared.
  • Perspective 3D Projection: The ring plane normal and centroid are computed in 3D world space. The renderer traces the inscribed dashed ring in-plane and projects translucent π electron cloud lobes at ±0.38 Å along the normal with conjugated orbital couplings.
  • Smooth Dynamic Transitions: If aromaticity is disrupted during electrophilic attack (forming a non-aromatic Wheland carbocation intermediate) and later restored upon deprotonation, the Π cloud smoothly fades out and fades in across step transitions.

4.5 Implicit Hydrogens and Automatic Valence Completion (Implicit Hydrogens)

When authoring organic reaction mechanisms, specifying every single C–H bond can be repetitive. CCPL supports implicit hydrogen abbreviation. You can declare only the skeletal carbon or heteroatom topology; the engine will automatically calculate and attach missing hydrogen atoms and single covalent bonds based on standard elemental valence, formal charges (charge), and unpaired radical electrons (radical):

Group Category Base Valence Correction Formula Examples
Group 14 (C, Si, Ge) 4 4 - |charge| - radical - (existing bond sum) Neutral C completes up to 4 bonds; carbocation C+ completes 3 bonds; methyl radical C· completes 3 bonds.
Group 15 (N, P, As) 3 3 + charge - radical - (existing bond sum) (Hypervalent P supported) Neutral amine N completes 3 bonds; quaternary ammonium N+ completes 4 bonds; amide anion N- completes 2 bonds.
Group 16 (O, S, Se, Te) 2 2 + charge - radical - (existing bond sum) (Hypervalent S supported) Neutral O completes 2 bonds; oxonium cation O+ completes 3 bonds; alkoxide anion O- completes 1 bond.
Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I) 1 max(0, 1 + charge - radical - existing bond sum) Neutral halogen completes 1 bond; bromonium cation Br+ completes 2 bonds.
Group 13 (B) 3 3 - charge - radical - (existing bond sum) Neutral B completes 3 bonds; borate anion B- completes 4 bonds.
Metals & Noble Gases 0 No implicit hydrogens added Transition metals (Fe, Cu, Pd, Pt) and alkali/alkaline earth ions retain user-specified coordination topology.

Implicit hydrogen control directives and modifiers:

  • hydrogens <auto|none|explicit>: Step-level or global directive. Defaults to auto; setting to none or explicit disables automatic hydrogen completion for the step.
  • atom <ID> <Element> explicit (or noImplicitH): Explicitly suppresses hydrogen completion on individual atoms, leaving bare radical sites or unsatisfied coordination positions.
💡 Toolbar "Complete H": Clicking the "Complete H" button in the deduction studio toolbar (or pressing Alt + H) converts the current script's implicit hydrogens into explicit atom H... and bond ... 1 declarations in the editor, allowing further manual edits or individual proton-transfer steps.

5. Polymer Repeat Units and Brackets (Polymer)

For polymerization reactions, bracketed repeat units can be declared via the polymer statement:

# Syntax: polymer "<subscript>" ["tag"] [include "<atom IDs...>" | exclude "<byproduct IDs...>"]
polymer "n" "Amylose Monomer Unit · [C_6H_{10}O_5]_n" include "C1 C2 C3 C4 C5 C6 O2 O3 O5 O6"

Mechanisms & Syntax:

  • Unit Specification (include / exclude): Use include to list the atoms within the repeating backbone, or use exclude to omit elimination byproducts (such as water or HCl molecules). Either option may be used.
